# Selene town quality: research and assessment

## Executive recommendation

**Do not replace Selene wholesale with a downloaded town.** The brief defines this as an AI-built visual demo, and its approved look is an original, welcoming soft-cinematic anime town: cream rounded architecture, petrol-blue trim, coral accents, substantial props, vegetation, and warm/cool cinematic light. A turnkey environment would make the project less original and probably require extensive palette, scale, terrain, road, and lighting rework. It would also conflict with the brief's default of producing assets through the approved Tripo/Blender pipeline. A modular pack can still be a useful optional kit or visual benchmark if the user approves the asset/license choice.

The strongest path is a **hybrid rebuild around the existing layout**: author one polished 40 × 30 m plaza/café vignette; unify roads, paving, and terrain; then combine a small modular architecture kit with existing Tripo landmarks and district-aware dressing. Keep the five-area plan, but make the 480 × 400 m field feel inhabited through terrain shaping, tighter views, layered silhouettes, and meaningful transitions. The blockout has 57 planned lots, but the current dressing plan lists just 48 props concentrated around the plaza and café.

If buying one pack is approved, **Stylized Japanese Street** is the closest fit. Use it as reusable pieces, not a drop-in town. Fab presently shows $22.49 sale / $44.99 list; its listing describes 133 modular assets and material/vertex-paint controls, but omits UE minor version, Nanite/Lumen claim, Mac status, and install size. Confirm before buying. Free City Sample Buildings is a useful modular reference, but its photoreal style does not fit Selene.

## 1. Diagnosis: why the town reads sparse and artificial

**Direct evidence.** `Saved/Checkpoint1/simple/12_aerial_overview.png` shows a large, nearly uniform green ground plane, long empty gaps between zones, flat gray road strips, pale rectangular pads, repeated mid-rise masses, and few small trees/props. `11_overlook_to_town.png` shows broad paving surfaces meeting at sharp boundaries and a town pushed far into the distance. These checkpoint/blockout images show unfinished presentation, not a failed final art pass. `Design/Blockout/map.png` documents 57 planned lots (10 hero facades) across 480 × 400 m; `dressing_map.png` and `dressing.json` show 48 prop placements and five optional building replacements. Dressing is route-focused, and the blockout README says its geometry makes no finished-art claim.

- **Scale and density:** a 480 × 400 m footprint with 57 lots spreads buildings across a large area. At aerial scale, clusters are separated by large empty fields. The screenshot's few close-set residential pieces don't provide enough mid-distance silhouettes to make those gaps feel intentional. Current plan density is not the same as visible finished density.
- **Terrain/layout logic:** the data defines a north ramp from 0 to 28 m plus many explicit flat pads, terrace elevations, and narrow roads. The checkpoint overview reads more like a grass plane with stepped pads than a continuous hillside; broad areas do not visually explain drainage, retaining edges, cuts/fills, paths, or why each neighborhood sits at its elevation. **Inference:** the height function is structurally useful for walkability, but without sculpted landforms and retaining/transition details it produces a model-like terrain read.
- **Conflicting road surfaces:** `ground_materials.json` assigns warm cream paving to slabs, petrol-gray cobble to every street, and grass to the full terrain; its notes explicitly say there are no dedicated road-border actors or terrain masks, and height textures are not used for displacement. `11_overlook_to_town.png` makes the paving/cobble boundary a large high-contrast seam. No curb, gutter, sidewalk shoulder, road-edge blend, patch, or crossing treatment is apparent in the image. The issue is not simply the chosen textures; their scale, color contrast, geometry, and hard boundaries compete.
- **Repetition and weak transitions:** the overview shows repeated similar facades and large bare buffers. The plan includes five districts, but the current render doesn't yet give each a distinct edge condition, foreground planting, alley/service layer, or skyline transition. The route-dressing map itself says pending envelopes need verification in Unreal.
- **Missing visual grounding:** the checkpoint capture has simple lighting, small sparse greenery, weak contact/detail cues at distance, and no visible road wear, decals, street furniture rhythm, utility lines, signage clusters, parked everyday objects, or people. Those absences flatten scale and make the view resemble a graybox/demo scene. The approved style calls for warm key/cool fill, dimensional shading, readable material response, and substantial props; this capture does not yet show that look.

**Existing authored assets are a useful base:** `Content/Assets/` already contains shop bakery and tool-repair buildings, townhouse/apartment/workshop/clock-hall forms, plaza furniture, plants, street trees, scooters, lamps, vending machine and other props. `Content/Selene/Materials/Ground/` has four imported ground-material folders. The current obstacle is more about a coherent assembly and authored landscape/material transition pass than a total lack of asset types.

## 3. Build-it-better without buying a town

1. **Fix the representative view first.** Keep the layout and concentrate effort on the plaza/café route the brief already designates as its look-development checkpoint. Dress the café frontage, clock landmark, shop edges, viaduct underside and one clear route into the interior. Make a daylight still and a moving gameplay-camera pass before propagating the design. Do not fill all 480 × 400 m with detail at once.
2. **Unify ground and streets.** Build one layered landscape material with a controlled grass/soil/stone/paving palette, slope/height masks and hand-authored masks for plaza, sidewalk and road. Use one consistent road family, then vary roughness, color and small patches rather than abruptly switching unrelated tiling materials. Add curb/gutter geometry and narrow edge strips, road-edge/grime decals, crossings, drains and threshold transitions. Use road/landscape splines for consistent width, grade, shoulders and curbs; use mesh decals where supported. Add RVT blending so terrain can receive road/prop shading and soften intersections. Unreal documents RVT specifically for landscape shading and blending with spline roads and decal-like materials. [RVT documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/runtime-virtual-texturing-in-unreal-engine) [Virtual texturing feature support](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/supported-features-by-rendering-path-for-desktop-with-unreal-engine)
3. **Make terrain tell a story.** Retain walkable, measured grades but sculpt broad landforms around the pads: slope breaks, shallow drainage swales, rock/retaining walls, terrace planting beds, stairs/ramps and believable cut/fill transitions. Add a distant forest/cliff/bay silhouette behind the town, then hide hard map edges with a continuous embankment/backdrop. The hillside should visibly explain the five stepped residential rows rather than read as a green plane with isolated height platforms.
4. **Use PCG for controlled repetition.** Use UE 5.8 PCG/editor tools for deterministic editor-time placement of grass/flowers, street trees, planters, poles, utility details, façade variants and small clutter constrained by area masks and road-clearance splines. Keep landmarks, hero storefronts, intersections, major terrain transitions and the camera route hand-authored. Bake generated output to instances/packed actors for runtime; avoid runtime generation for this static 480 m demo unless interaction needs it. PCG supports spline/surface/volume authoring; Epic's sample demonstrates spline and forest use. [PCG editor mode](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/pcg-editor-mode-in-unreal-engine) [Electric Dreams guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/electric-dreams-environment-in-unreal-engine)
5. **Set dressing by district, not random fill.** Use storefront awnings/signs/seating; hillside stairs, retaining walls, planters and laundry; mid-rise balconies and courtyard furniture; industrial crates, cables and loading markings; plaza lamps, benches and banners. As a starting camera-density heuristic per ~100 m visible frontage: plaza 3–5 hero anchors plus 15–25 medium props and 30–50 small/foliage accents; commercial 6–10 storefront beats plus 10–16 street props; each hillside terrace one retaining/planting composition and 2–4 door/lane details; mid-rise 4–6 courtyard/utility clusters; depot 5–8 yard anchors plus 10–15 small service details. Tune by view and budget, avoid repeated identical clusters, and kitbash missing wall/roof/trim/sign/curb pieces around Tripo heroes. Use PCG district/biome rules for vegetation and street props; keep hero buildings and intersections hand-authored.
6. **Protect style and performance.** For style B, use warm key light, cool shadow fill, controlled exposure and restrained bloom; keep surface roughness and texture scale consistent and add contact/occlusion cues where assets meet ground. Review noon, dusk, night and interior transitions. The brief expects dynamic day/night lighting; on this Mac, profile real packaged builds and camera routes against the 60 fps requirement instead of assuming asset marketing claims apply. Epic lists Nanite as beta-supported on Apple Silicon M2+ and TSR as supported, so use Nanite selectively and keep LOD/instancing options. [UE 5.8 macOS requirements](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/tr-tr/unreal-engine/macos-development-requirements-for-unreal-engine)

**Workflow effort estimate (inference):** 2–4 focused agent-days for the route/material/lighting foundation; 5–10 for five districts, modular pieces, dressing, and selected interiors; 2–3 for cleanup, route review, and packaged profiling. Rough total: **9–17 agent-days**, assuming the blockout and Tripo pipeline remain usable. Main uncertainty is modular reuse and style/material rework; this is effort, not a calendar promise.

## 4. Recommendation and phased plan

**Recommended path: rebuild the presentation as an original authored town using existing Tripo assets plus a small Blender modular kit and selective PCG; do not buy a whole-town replacement now.** This keeps the approved AI-built premise, preserves the bespoke clock/plaza/hillside plan, and addresses the visual failure directly. The existing `shop-bakery-corner`, `shop-tool-repair`, `townhouse-terrace`, `apartment-curved`, `workshop-garage`, `clock-hall`, plants, lamps and street props are enough to begin a designed route. A purchased modular street may be considered later as a controlled test if it has clear replacement value.

**Phases:**

1. **Composition and ground (largest immediate win):** select the plaza/café route camera views; settle one cohesive road/paving/curb material language; improve hillside and road-to-terrain edges; add strong foreground/midground layers and a readable distant skyline. First visual approval should be from eye level, not only aerial.
2. **Style checkpoint:** finish the plaza/café route with the brief's cream/petrol/coral palette, façade depth, windows, signage, plants, furniture, subtle wear and warm/cool light. Compare day, dusk, and night and measure packaged Mac performance with the expected crowd level before expanding.
3. **District kits:** build a small grid-aligned modular architecture/curb/retaining-wall/sign kit in Blender, retaining Tripo-made distinctive props and hero forms. Populate backgrounds and district-specific mid-scale elements using deterministic PCG; hand-author the hero blocks and view corridors.
4. **Expand and tune:** propagate accepted kits across residential, commercial, plaza, and industrial zones; then finish the overlook and backdrop. Use Packed Level Actors/instances for repeated structures where beneficial, and revisit 60 fps in packaged captures as density grows.

**Risks:** a downloaded environment may bring incompatible content, unwanted style, heavy materials, and conversion work; showcase performance does not prove Mac performance. Mac Nanite is beta-supported. PCG can amplify repetition without neighborhood-aware rules. Road/terrain edits must preserve route grades, capsule clearance, and day/night continuity.

**Open questions for the user:**

- Is spending acceptable for one modular kit if the first route style test shows a clear time/quality gain? If yes, shortlist Stylized Japanese Street first; confirm Fab's live price, tier and exact UE 5.8.4/Mac compatibility before purchase.
- Is using licensed third-party modular content acceptable within the “AI-built town demo” premise, provided the landmark assets and final composition remain Selene's own? Buying and downloading are not part of this research-only task.
- Should the town keep the full 480 × 400 m play area, or may the art pass use distant set dressing/occlusion and tighter camera framing to make the inhabited core feel more compact?
